Chapter 15. Day-by-day plan

This chapter turns the guide into a calendar. It assumes you have 6 to 8 hours a day and plans between 3 and 7 of them, which leaves room for surprises, and it fits the whole build and the evidence into seven working days plus one spare day. If you only have 3 or 4 hours a day, the second half of the chapter shows how to stretch the same plan over about two weeks without changing the order. There are no commands here. Read it on day 1, right after chapter 0, then add a "Planned day" column to the progress tracker from chapter 0 and fill it in from the table below.

The plan's order and its terms

The plan puts whatever is most likely to fail on your laptop first, so the cluster and Cilium come on day 1, and chapter 1, which has no commands, waits until day 2. The go/no-go point is the moment you decide whether to continue on WSL or switch to the Ubuntu virtual machine in appendix A, and it falls on the evening of day 1. A buffer day is a day with no planned chapter, kept free for whatever slipped.

Each day ends with a "done when" line. It names the checkpoint that must match before you stop for the day. If it doesn't match, the next day starts with fixing it, because every chapter builds on the one before.

The seven working days add up to about 33 to 41 hours, plus the buffer day. The times below come from the chapter headers and from the test run of this guide. Your first day will probably run longer than planned, since installing software is where most surprises happen, and later days tend to run shorter once the tools are familiar. Days 2 and 7 are planned shorter than the rest, and the spare time on day 2 is for whatever day 1 left over.

What to do if day 1 fails

If the connectivity test in chapter 4 still fails after you've worked through its failure blocks and chapter 13, stop trying to fix WSL. Start appendix A the same evening. Setting up the virtual machine takes about 2 hours, and chapters 3 and 4 then take another 3 to 4. That costs you most of a day, which is what the buffer day is for. Everything from chapter 5 onward is the same in the virtual machine.

One failure has nothing to do with your laptop. Some networks block certain internet addresses, and then only the tests that reach the internet fail. Chapter 4 shows how to recognise that case and suggests trying a phone hotspot before you give up on WSL.

Using day 8, the buffer day

Spend day 8 on whatever slipped. Start with any checkpoint that didn't match, then rerun the chapter 14 checkpoint and capture whatever it reports missing. If time is left, redraw the result charts from your own numbers as chapter 12 shows and back up ~/microseg with the tar command from chapter 2. Use whatever time remains to start the results chapter of your thesis while the screenshots are fresh.

The plan at 3 to 4 hours a day

At half the daily time, every day of the plan becomes two, except day 7, which fits in one. Keep the same order and split each day where the table below says, so every sitting ends at a natural stopping point.

That makes about 15 days, the two buffer days included. The go/no-go point moves to the end of day 2, and it's still the first thing you find out.

Chapter 3 at a short sitting can end halfway through the installs. That's safe as long as you stop after a step whose "You should see" matched, because every install step in chapter 3 can be repeated without harm. Write down in the progress tracker which step you stopped at.

If time collapses: what to cut first

If a deadline moves closer and you can't finish everything, cut in this order. In chapter 7, paste both dashboard files as usual but skip the route tests in steps 4, 6 and 7. After step 3, read step 5, which shows how one probe works, then go on to steps 8 and 9 and the checkpoint. You still get the full dashboard, so nothing later changes, and you save an hour or two. If the checkpoint fails, go back and run the skipped tests, because they show which route is broken. Then skip the three break-it drills in chapter 13, reading them without doing them. After that, drop the Hubble UI screenshots E18 and E30, because the text outputs E26 to E29 prove the same thing. The chapter 14 checkpoint then prints missing E18 png, missing E30 png and 2 missing, followed by 39 and 37, and after this cut that counts as a pass.

Never cut chapter 10, chapter 11, the toggle test or the evidence checkpoint, because your results come from them. And don't skip a checkpoint to save time, because a problem you carry forward is much harder to find two chapters later.
